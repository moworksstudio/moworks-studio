# Ideas by kind of film (none of this is a rule)

From the Moworks Motion kit 2.0.4 (`workflow/recipes.md`). What worked on past films: take what helps THIS film, ignore the rest, break it
when the idea is better. The groups disagree with each other on purpose (what suits a launch is wrong for a premium product). The owner's
words beat all of it. Library items named here are found with the library search; HyperFrames' motion craft is in its `references/motion.md`.

## 1. Energy (youth, launch, social, music)

For young, playful, launch and music films (the owner's words young, lively, dynamic, "not tame" point here too). A tame first cut was the
slowest part of an earlier film (six rounds to get here).
- **One type scale for every super** (a guideline: its own sizes); the montage words (the hook words, the chant, the one-word punches)
  break it, in size AND colour. Big one-word type may sit UNDER the product while it reads.
- **Colour on the montage words**, from the brand's or product's own colours where there are some, on a background it reads on. With a
  guideline, "many colours" in a brief means more of the brand's colours; with none, your own palette.
- **Every montage word its own entrance** (stomp, spin, drop, zoom, flip, rise, slide), never one shared move; then a kinetic exit.
- **A sticker / icon family drawn for the film** (pixel-art, line icons, cut paper: one style), one per feature or idea, popping on
  `steps()`, bobbing on the beat.
- **Beat punctuation:** a ring opening from the word or the hero, a burst on the hits, never over text.
- **Graphic transitions that run UNDER the hero** (stripes, a block wipe, a circle from the hero): the hero carries every cut.
- **Colour bars swept in behind key numbers;** feature words in word by word.
- **Ambient life clear of the text:** shapes drifting only on free spots, at least 60 px from every text box.
- A product film: one product through the film (one element living across shots), a light sweep on its turns, glows on film-time waves.
- **Liveliness quotas** (the best-liked test film met them): a shot change or a new world at least every 3 s; nothing still for more than
  0.5 s (supers only pulse while read); every montage word its own entrance AND its own size / weight / colour; one sticker per feature,
  bouncing on the beat; a background that is never a flat colour for long (patterns drifting, rings blooming, soft blobs, 30-60 particles
  fading near text).
- **Always-on layers** written once for the whole film, not per shot, so they never stop at a cut.

## 2. Premium product (calm, high-end, SaaS, corporate)

- Hook in the first 2 s; something new every 2-4 s; no shot where nothing happens.
- One display font + one UI font, one accent colour (unless the brand says otherwise).
- Avoid: a title on a gradient with everything fading in; small labels in the corners and decorative frames; glow / gradient on UI edges;
  generic particle bursts; bouncy easing on text; icons with mismatched stroke weights.
- Text inside a frame that is changing shape: in after the change starts, out before the next one.
- Never fade black straight to the accent colour: carry an accent-coloured element from one shot into the next.
- Springs: UI may overshoot a little, text not.

## 3. UI morph (app, software, a product with a screen)

- **One shape never cuts:** it changes size, corner radius and colour through 8-12 states (button → card → screen → notification...).
- **A cursor drives it** with real clicks, drags and typing; the camera zooms so each state fills the frame; the end can loop to the start.
- **Five beats:** the hook (the problem in 5 big words) → the product assembling → 3 features, each with the cursor → 1 number that proves
  it → logo + call to action.
- **Real UI, real data:** the client's screenshots or screen recordings (from the assets, or captured from their site when the project
  allows the web) in device frames from the library; never draw a fake UI of the client's product.

## 4. Song film (a lyric film, a launch cut to a track)

The best-synced test film followed the beat (kick, bass) and the lyrics precisely.
- **One grid for everything:** the song's beat map (the app runs HyperFrames' beat analysis) drives the shots, the words, the hits and, when
  the owner wants a groove, the pulses.
- **Every sung word on its sung time** (from the transcript of the vocal, checked by the owner); montage words on beats.
- **Land ON the word:** the impact (a stomp reaching size, a fold closing) falls on the sung onset, so the move STARTS before it (about
  0.07 s for a 0.25 s stomp). A move that only starts on the word is felt late.
- **Moves fit the note:** on eighth notes (0.22 s at 135 BPM) a word's entrance lasts at most one eighth.
- Shots = the song's sections (hook, pre-drop, drop, verse, outro); cut on downbeats.
- The lyric is the copy: show the sung line, word by word; a claim placed in a lyric gap still gets its reading time.
- A held note can be drawn (letters added one by one until the note ends).
- **Palette flip:** the same layout re-skins on a section change by switching its colour variables in 0 ms on the downbeat.

## 5. Hand-drawn (kids, education, playful brands, explainers with a human touch)

- Two looks, pick one per film: **brush ink + watercolour** (lively, warm, a little imperfect: tapered brush lines that boil while they
  hold, watercolour washes blooming from a point, one accent red) or **textured editorial** (calm, precise: drawings held perfectly still,
  acting on twos, light bands, film grain).
- **Pencil marks** for circling, underlining, crossing out, sketch guides (edges overshoot the corners).
- **Everything breathes** (a boil about 10 times a second); entrances on `steps()` eases.
- **Impacts:** a frozen squash at the hit + one flash frame, a decaying shake, a comic sound word slammed in.
- Avoid: glossy gradients, glows, perfect vector strokes next to hand-drawn ones, motion blur on drawings.

## 6. Graphics over a talking head (interviews, founder videos, B-roll)

HyperFrames' `references/overlays.md` and `captions.md` hold its measured facts for cards over a person speaking; in the General style:
- **Density first:** about 3 graphics per 30 s (medium) or 4-5 (heavy). Heavy is easier to cut down than to add to.
- **Word timings first** (the transcript): every graphic lands on its spoken word; one timing file drives the captions and the cues.
- **Plan line by line, each graphic tagged:** full cutaway (3-10 s, at least 2 s of face between) · face stays, a panel in the empty side of
  the frame · lower third.
- **On 9:16:** the speaker at the bottom, one graphic at the top, the captions; nothing else.
- **Captions:** 1-3 words popping, or 3-7 in karaoke; at least 0.7 s on screen, at most about 17 characters a second; a word lights on its
  first frame, never before. Vietnamese: pick a caption style whose fonts draw every diacritic.
