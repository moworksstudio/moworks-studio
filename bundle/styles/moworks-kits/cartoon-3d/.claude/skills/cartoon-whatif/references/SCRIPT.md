# Writing a "What If…?" story (16:9)

These are our craft rules for second-person "What If…?" stories, in plain words, for 30–50 s pieces and 8–12 min
episodes. Write your own premise beats, lines, gags and characters inside them. If the user shows a video they like,
it teaches the ladder only, never its lines, jokes, characters, recurring devices, tagline or title styling.

## The voice in numbers
| | Target |
|---|---|
| Speed | 160–185 words a minute (≈ 2.7–3.1 words/s); the format tables budget ≈ 2.8–3.0 to leave room for gags |
| Clauses | 6–9 words each, joined into one paragraph; the longest sentences about 20 words; 2–4-word punches allowed as the kicker |
| Person | second person, present tense; "you/your" ≈ 15 % of all words |
| Side characters (episodes) | a short line about every 25 s, 2–8 words, each character in its own fixed voice |
| Beats (episodes) | a new image, place or gag about every 15–20 s; a spoken signpost every 30–45 s |
| Numbers | clustered in the first 60 %; the story half has almost none |

## The 13 principles
| # | Principle | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Premise = one dial turned up, or one new verb.** "What if you were N× ⟨one measurable quality⟩?" (stronger, faster, smaller) or "What if you could ⟨verb⟩?". Pick a power that collides with ordinary days: school, work, food, transport. A multiplier gives free sequels (10× → 100× → 1,000×) and maths beats | one change fits in a title and leaves room for consequences |
| 2 | **Cold open = an ordinary moment broken by the power.** 10–30 s in an episode, second person, present tense, a mundane setting. The power shows up by accident and a bystander gets a 2–6-word reaction (or just a face). Then the title question is spoken ("So, what if you…?"), followed at once by a "here's how it works" signpost | the viewer sees the power before hearing it named, and the spoken title confirms they're in the right video |
| 3 | **Rules before fun.** 1–3 rules, numbered aloud, each proved by a small gag. Say what does *not* change (strength ×100, but your weight and grip stay the same). **Plant one rule out loud** ("remember that") so it can bite later | rules make every later consequence feel earned; the plant is a promise the viewer waits for |
| 4 | **"It's real, kind of."** Within the first 1–2 min, ground the power in 2–4 real anchors: an experiment, a record, an animal, a known effect. Numbers come as scale comparisons ("about X, faster than Y") | one true thing makes the fiction believable, and the viewer learns something early |
| 5 | **The ladder: cool → useful → inconvenient → dangerous → existential.** Cool and useful are a 45–90 s montage of daily-life wins. Inconvenient is 3–4 **numbered problems**, each with a physics or biology reason, a funny picture and a workaround. Dangerous is the "real problem" pivot near 40 %, where the planted rule bites. Existential is who you become: loneliness, temptation, ageing, loyalty | each rung raises the stakes, so attention climbs with the runtime |
| 6 | **Every problem leaves a tool, and the climax uses the tools.** A workaround (gear, a technique, an animal that already solved it) becomes the move that wins, or tempts, in the climax. The climax tests the rules, not raw power | the ending feels clever, not random |
| 7 | **Realism beats get a flag phrase.** "This part is real", "this has actually been measured", "and no, that's not made up". Every such line is marked `verify` | it answers the doubt before it reaches the comments |
| 8 | **A moral fork near 60–75 %.** The power meets someone else's safety. You either become a hero (a careful, ironic rescue) or slide into villainy step by step | the story gets a heart and a choice the viewer argues about |
| 9 | **The twist: the world gets bigger.** The last ~20 % opens a bigger story: the series' recurring device shows up, a past character returns, and a new threat appears in the last 30 s, unresolved. The device is our own, invented with the user (cartoon-whatif SKILL.md, "Series spine") | episodes connect, so viewers go on to the next one |
| 10 | **Attention every 20–40 s.** A new beat (image, place or gag) every ~15–20 s, with shots changing every 3–6 s inside it; a spoken signpost every 30–45 s ("But then…", "Problem two…", "And then, one Friday…"); open a loop ("it matters later") and close it 2–4 min later; a side-character line about every 25 s; callbacks to earlier gags | something new always arrives before attention drifts |
| 11 | **The voice: a friend narrating your life.** Second person, present tense, contractions, short spoken units, dry asides to the viewer ("please don't try this"), understatement and one absurdly specific image. Written as **one continuous paragraph** of 6–9-word clauses joined by and / so / because / but; at most one or two real fragments (e.g. the kicker question) | it sounds told, not read |
| 12 | **The ending: a two-question sign-off, ≤ 20 s** (episodes). First the power question ("what would you do first?"), then one story question (who's behind it, would you go back). Then ask for comments and say the story goes on. No recap | comments come from a question the viewer wants to answer |
| 13 | **Packaging.** The title is the question, 4–9 words, with a multiplier when there is one, in normal title case. The description, tags and YouTube chapters follow FORMATS.md B; idea-lab writes them | the title promises exactly the one change the video delivers |

## What we deliberately do
- **Our "you" is a cast member on screen**, Mika by default, not a faceless figure. Dialogue lines go to Ravi, June,
  Otto, or new originals made with the character-builder skill.
- **No other people's IP** in jokes: no film, game, anime or brand references. The jokes come from the story's world.
- **No real people named or drawn.** Records and experiments are described without faces or names.
- **Normal title case:** "What If You Were 100× Stronger?". Never "What IF", never all caps.
- **Short pieces (30–50 s) compress the same ladder:** one rung per 5–8 s, and a callback kicker instead of the
  universe twist (FORMATS.md A).
- **The premise is fiction**, and the script says where the real part starts ("Some people are born this way", "This
  part is real").

## Fact rules
- **Every real-science claim** gets a `verify:` note in the draft: what to check and where (a medical reference, a
  record-keeping body, a paper, a reputable news report).
- **The facts table** lives in `projects/<slug>/FACTS.md`: claim · as spoken · source · checked date. Lines inside the
  premise are marked "fiction".
- **On screen:** only what the voice says, word for word, numbers included.
- **Numbers** only when a source gives them, said with "about". Never invent a statistic to make a beat land; use a
  comparison picture instead ("faster than the bus", "hotter than the coffee pot").
- **Unverifiable** → attribute it ("some researchers think…") or cut it, and list it in the delivery.
- `projects/<slug>/script.txt` holds the spoken words only; the `verify:` notes stay in the draft and FACTS.md.

## Worked example: "What if you stopped feeling pain?" (45 s table, 131 words, 49.7 s)
The script of `src/examples/what-if-no-pain/`:

> On Monday you kick the desk leg so hard the whole office winces, but you don't feel a thing. So, what if you
> stopped feeling pain? Some people are born this way, with a rare condition called congenital insensitivity to
> pain: they can tell hot from cold, it just never hurts. At first that's a superpower, because flu shots are nothing
> and you pour the team's coffee from the scorching pot, bare-handed. Then you notice the blisters, and you bite your
> tongue at lunch and keep chewing, and you sprain an ankle and keep running, because nothing tells you to stop. Your
> appendix could burst and you'd just feel off, because pain was never the damage, it's the alarm. And that desk leg
> on Monday? Your toe's been broken all week.

| Beat (A2) | Voice time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0–5.3 s | an ordinary Monday; the team winces, you don't (the planted detail: the kick) |
| Title question | 6.2–8.5 s | "So, what if you stopped feeling pain?", written on the meeting-room whiteboard |
| Rule + this is real | 9.7–19.8 s | the real condition (`verify`: a medical genetics reference); what stays normal: hot and cold |
| Cool → useful | 20.7–27.8 s | the flu shot; pouring the scorching pot for the team |
| Inconvenient | 28.4–38.3 s | blisters, a bitten tongue, running on a sprain, joined into one sentence |
| Dangerous | 38.9–41.1 s | a silent appendix ("could": hedged, sourced) |
| Reframe | 41.8–44.1 s | "pain was never the damage, it's the alarm" |
| Kicker | 45.1–48.7 s | the callback: the Monday toe was broken all week |

It has no numbers at all, and every medical line has a source in its FACTS.md. That's the bar.

## Script checklist (run before any voice is made)
- [ ] The title is a "What if you…?" question, 4–9 words, with one dial or one verb, in normal title case
- [ ] Cold open: an ordinary moment, second person, present tense, a bystander reaction; the title question follows
      at once (pieces: inside the format table's slot; episodes: by ≈ 5 % of the runtime)
- [ ] 1–3 rules stated; what stays normal is stated; **one planted rule or detail** that pays off later
- [ ] At least one "this is real" beat, with every factual line marked `verify:` + where to check it
- [ ] The ladder in order: cool → useful → inconvenient → dangerous → existential/moral (pieces: cool → useful →
      inconvenient → dangerous → reframe → kicker)
- [ ] Each problem = reason + funny picture + workaround; the climax (or kicker) uses an earlier rule or callback
- [ ] Attention: a new picture every ≤ 20 s (≤ 4 s in pieces), a signpost every 30–45 s, a short side-character line
      about every 25 s in episodes
- [ ] **One flowing paragraph** (one per chapter in episodes): clauses of 6–9 words joined by and / so / because /
      but; "you/your" in most clauses; contractions; no list longer than three
- [ ] Words: 30 s ≈ 80–85 · 40 s ≈ 105–115 · 45 s ≈ 120–135 · 50 s ≈ 135–145 · 10 min ≈ 1,600–1,750 (the low end
      with a generated voice)
- [ ] Episodes end with the twist plus a two-question sign-off (≤ 20 s); pieces end on the kicker (an optional
      ≤ 8-word question after it)
- [ ] Nothing from another creator: no lines, gags, characters, organisation, tagline, recurring device or title
      styling; our cast only
- [ ] Every sentence maps to a shot in the shot list (FORMATS.md)
