On Monday you kick the desk leg so hard the whole office winces, but you don't feel a thing. So, what if you stopped feeling pain? Some people are born this way, with a rare condition called congenital insensitivity to pain: they can tell hot from cold, it just never hurts. At first that's a superpower, because flu shots are nothing and you pour the team's coffee from the scorching pot, bare-handed. Then you notice the blisters, and you bite your tongue at lunch and keep chewing, and you sprain an ankle and keep running, because nothing tells you to stop. Your appendix could burst and you'd just feel off, because pain was never the damage, it's the alarm. And that desk leg on Monday? Your toe's been broken all week.
