# The "what if" script formula

A Stickman Explainer is a premise ("What if you could fly?") told in **second person, present tense, in fragments**.
It moves fast, every line is a picture, and every number is real. Write your own story and lines inside this
structure.

## The numbers
| | Target |
|---|---|
| Length | short: 35–60 s (about 80–140 spoken words) · long: 6–12 min (about 900–1,500 words) |
| Pace | about 165 words a minute while speaking (2.6–2.8 words/s inside phrases), with real pauses of 0.3–0.5 s between lines; the gaps carry the beats |
| Sentences | about 5 words on average, half of them 4 words or fewer: fragments are the norm |
| Person | "you" and "your" throughout; the hero is named only by other characters |
| Questions | at the title, the thesis and the cliffhanger |
| Shots | one spoken line = one picture, about 1.5 s each in the cold open |

## Short form (35–60 s): the worked example's shape
| Part | Job | "What If You Could Fly?" (the kit's example) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Cold open (≈ 12 s) | the power used casually, as small staccato proofs, ending on a witness | "Eight lanes. Nobody's moved in twenty minutes. You're already at your desk. You left through your bedroom window. Two rooftops. One very confused pigeon. Ninety seconds, window to desk." · coworker: "Did you just come in through the window?" |
| 2 · Title card (≈ 3 s) | WHAT IF YOU COULD / **FLY?** with a boom, no narration | |
| 3 · Grounding (≈ 11 s) | real numbers that climb, from a familiar reference to the premise's scale | "The high jump world record? Two point four five meters. Skydivers jump from about four kilometers. Airliners cruise around eleven." |
| 4 · The payoff image | the premise at its most absurd and calm | "And somewhere above that, you're eating a sandwich." |
| 5 · The catch | the cost of the power, made physical | "At around minus fifty-five degrees." |
| 6 · The twist / hook | the problem the power creates, then out | "Turns out flying is the easy part. Everything else is a problem." |

## Long form (6–12 min)
1. **Cold open (≈ 15 s, about 40 words).** Four or five proof vignettes, one per shot, staccato and escalating.
   Then the stakes, then a small domestic kicker.
2. **Title card (≈ 3 s).** It gets a boom and no narration.
3. **Grounding in real numbers (≈ 15 s).** A normal reference point, then rare, then the limit of measurement, then
   the premise. The camera travels through a data metaphor: a bell curve, a ruler, a map.
4. **First character beat, then the thesis.** A two-line exchange with someone close to the hero, then a
   one-sentence rule of the premise ("Your body is normal. Your mind is not.").
5. **The promise of duality.** Make it "Amazing. And awful. Usually both at once."
6. **Plus / minus alternation (≈ 3 min).** About ten consequences, each 15–30 s and flagged by a corner badge
   (yellow **+**, grey **−**). Each one contains:
   - a claim
   - a two-line micro-scene with dialogue
   - one real science fact where it fits
   - **seeds** for later callbacks: three or four specific images planted early

   It ends on the cost.
7. **The turn.** An emotional anchor, built from three or four concrete details told in short lines.
8. **The incident.** The world hurts the anchor, mostly in dialogue. Add one real explainer line, the power's limit,
   and an emotional low.
9. **The choice.** The easy dark option, then the moral line.
10. **Climax montage.** Staccato action, the villain's line, a restrained victory, a confrontation punchline.
11. **Resolution.** Pay off every seed.
12. **Sequel hook.** A message typed on screen from an unknown sender, then the ask (like, subscribe, part two), and
    a question in the description to draw comments ("What would YOU do?").

## Line rules
- **One idea per sentence.** Cut the connectives and let the picture carry the link.
- **Triads and lists of fragments** ("Two rooftops. One very confused pigeon.")
- **Numbers are anchors.** Say them exactly, and show them on screen exactly as said.
- **Callbacks are the payoff.** Plant specific images early and close every one.
- **Dialogue is brief**: one or two lines per character, then back to "you".
- **Every line names its shot.** If a line has no picture, cut it.
- **Write the narration as one continuous paragraph per act**, with dialogue marked
  `[NAME: "…"]` and the title marked `[TITLE]`.

## Facts
Every statistic or "science" line must be **true and sourced** before it's voiced. Keep a facts table in
`projects/<slug>/PLAN.md` (claim · the number as spoken · source · checked date). Round only the way the script
says it ("about four kilometres"), and flag anything you couldn't verify. The example's facts:

| Claim | As spoken | Source |
|---|---|---|
| High jump world record | 2.45 m | Javier Sotomayor, 1993; still the world record |
| Skydiving exit altitude | about 4 km | typical drop-zone exit altitudes are 10,000–14,000 ft |
| Airliner cruise | around 11 km | typical cruise is 30,000–40,000 ft |
| Air temperature up there | around −55 °C | the International Standard Atmosphere gives −56.5 °C from 11 to 20 km |
