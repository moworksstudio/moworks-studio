A needle is about to go into your arm. In a second, it will push ten drops of medicine through your skin. You can't just swallow it, because your stomach would digest many medicines, like insulin, the same way it digests lunch. So we take a shortcut, and where the needle stops decides how fast it works. In the fat under your skin, it seeps into your blood over hours. In muscle, packed with tiny blood vessels, it takes minutes, and in a vein, just seconds. Then your heart pumps it around your body in about a minute, all from one small sting.
